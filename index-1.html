1. SMILES notation

SMILES (Simplified Molecular Input Line Entry System) represents a molecule as text. Atoms are written using element symbols, while bonds and branches are represented by characters.

  • O — oxygen atom in a simple SMILES example.
  • CC — two carbon atoms connected by a single bond (ethane).
  • CCO — ethanol.
  • C1CCCCC1 — a six-membered carbon ring (cyclohexane).

2. Molecular representations

A 2D structure shows atoms and bonds on a flat page. A 3D model represents spatial positions and helps us think about molecular shape. A 3D model is a visual aid; the coordinates shown here are illustrative.

3. Molecular properties

Molecular mass is the sum of atomic masses. Polarity describes uneven distribution of electrical charge. Boiling point and solubility depend on multiple factors, including intermolecular forces, molecular shape, temperature, and solvent.

4. Structure–property relationships

Changing a molecule's atoms, functional groups, or shape can change its properties. For example, ethanol has an –OH group that can form hydrogen bonds, while methane is nonpolar. Trends must be interpreted in context rather than assumed to apply to every molecule.
